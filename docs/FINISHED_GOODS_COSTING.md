# Finished goods costing and valuation

Money is safe integer paisa. Snapshots distinguish `recordedPaisa`, `estimatedPaisa` and `incomplete`. Later master rates, invoices or display-name edits never rewrite an existing finished lot.

Component issue captures attributable source material/cutting scrap, recorded embroidery and approved production expenses. Upstream fabric/dyeing/printing/finishing cost is already inherited through the processed source and is not added again. Unbilled services/current-rate material remain estimates. An unclosed production order or missing price marks lineage incomplete; a recorded subset is not represented as a finalized full actual cost.

Assembly WIP carries the saved cost of its actual issued counts. Matching transfers the selected component portion cost and actual counted accessory consumption into its batch. Explicit returns carry value back. Rework replacements add replacement input and completed approved correction cost once; reusable removed components release their saved attributable cost, while scrap remains absorbed. Reinspection changes quality, not original matching quantity.

Counted materials use moving-average value with exact integer proportional consumption. A final depletion consumes the remaining value, avoiding a stranded paisa or negative quantity. Packaging completion snapshots its share of remaining manufacturing value, allocated material cost and owner-approved per-set labor/overhead. Rejected sets are excluded from the remaining accepted-output denominator; unresolved QC makes the allocation provisional/incomplete. Previously posted completion value stays frozen.

The original lot value is allocated across its current location/quality quantities using largest remainders, conserving all paisa on internal splits. Approved count gains/losses use the original lot's proportional unit-value basis; excess counts qualify valuation as incomplete. Manufacturing price/contribution comparisons are estimates, never realized sales or net profit.

Historical finished SKU portions use a qualified aggregate estimate from original batch material allocations and direct costs, apportioned once across that batch's packaged SKU quantities. Missing packaging-material/individual assembly lineage is always marked incomplete. They are not valued from current master rates and a production batch's whole cost is not copied to every color SKU.

Counted carton boxes used after finished receipt are warehouse handling consumption. Their expense evidence remains separate from the frozen manufacturing receipt value. Phase 10 should carry these distinctions into wholesale planning; Phase 12 may add a full accounting policy rather than silently capitalizing historical handling costs.
